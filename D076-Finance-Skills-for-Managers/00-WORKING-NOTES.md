# Unit 2 — Foundations of Finance (Form A)

## Key Concepts to Remember

### Areas of Finance
| Area | What it means | Remember |
|------|---------------|----------|
| **Investments** | Deciding which assets to invest in to create wealth in the future | Stocks, bonds, real estate, etc. — choosing assets for future wealth |
| **Financial management** | Managing a firm's finances / making firm-level financial decisions | Different from "investments" as a field |
| **Financial institutions** | Banks, insurance, pension funds, etc. that facilitate money flow | Intermediaries |
| **Asset pricing** | Determining the value/price of financial assets | Valuation focus |

### Goal of the Firm
- **Main goal of a firm = maximize owner wealth**
- Financial managers should make all decisions with this goal in mind
- Not just "make money" in the abstract — specifically owner/shareholder wealth

### Three Types of Financial Manager Decisions
| Decision type | What it is | Example |
|---------------|------------|---------|
| **Investment decision** | Evaluate whether spending money on something is worth it (use of investors' money) | Is R&D for a new product worth it? |
| **Financing decision** | How to raise/fund money for projects | Debt vs equity |
| **Working capital management** | Day-to-day cash flow management | Inventory, receivables, payables |

**Trap to avoid:** Evaluating R&D / projects = **investment decision**, NOT managing working capital.

### Financial Markets
| Market | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| **Primary market** | Securities sold for the **first time** (IPO, new bond issues) |
| **Secondary market** | Securities traded **after** initial issuance (stock exchange trading among investors) |

**Remember:** First sale = primary; after that = secondary.

### Types of Financial Institutions
| Type | Examples / Role |
|------|-----------------|
| **Depository** | Banks, credit unions — take deposits |
| **Contractual** | **Insurance companies** — contractual savings institutions |
| **Investment** | Investment banks, mutual funds (as investment vehicles) |
| **Pension fund** | Invests funds contributed by a company to provide **retirement funds** for employees |

**Remember:** Insurance = contractual; Pension fund = company retirement money for employees.

### Economic Indicators
| Type | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Leading** | Predict future economic changes |
| **Coincident** | Change **as** the economy changes (move with the economy) |
| **Lagging** | Change after the economy has already changed |

**Personal income = coincident indicator** (changes as the economy changes).

### Ethics
- **Ethical** = conforms with accepted standards of conduct that guide a person’s behavior
- Distinct from: legal (laws), moral (personal beliefs), standard (generic)

### Steps to Solve an Ethical Dilemma
1. **Identify and define the problem**
2. **Consider alternative courses of action** ← second step (tested)
3. Consider the consequences that may come from the action
4. (Continue toward choosing/implementing the best ethical action)

### Agency Problems & Corporate Control
- **Agency problem:** Conflict between managers (agents) and owners (principals)
- **How to reduce via corporate control:** **Executive compensation** — especially stocks and stock options
  - Aligns management with owners; can encourage taking value-creating (sometimes riskier) projects that increase owner wealth
- Setting stricter goals alone does **not** necessarily reduce agency problems

## Practice Questions (Unit 2 Form A) — with Correct Answers

**1.** Which area of finance involves deciding which assets to invest in to create wealth in the future?  
**Answer: Investments**  
Why: Investments = choosing assets to create future wealth.

**2.** What is the main goal of a firm?  
**Answer: To maximize owner wealth**  
Why: Financial managers decide based on maximizing owner/shareholder wealth.

**3.** What are financial managers doing if they evaluate whether it is worth spending money on research and development for a new product?  
**Answer: Making an investment decision**  
Why: Assessing costs/benefits of potential investments.  
**Wrong trap:** Managing working capital = day-to-day cash flows, not R&D evaluation.

**4.** Which type of financial market is where securities such as stocks and bonds are traded after their initial issuance?  
**Answer: The secondary financial market**  
Why: Primary = first sale; secondary = traded among investors afterward.

**5.** What type of financial institution is an insurance company?  
**Answer: Contractual**  
Why: Insurance companies are contractual savings institutions.

**6.** Which financial institution invests funds contributed by a company to provide retirement funds for the company’s employees?  
**Answer: Pension fund**  
Why: That is the role of pension funds.

**7.** Personal income is which type of economic indicator?  
**Answer: Coincident**  
Why: Coincident indicators change as the economy changes.

**8.** Which term refers to something that conforms with accepted standards of conduct that guide a person’s behavior?  
**Answer: Ethical**  
Why: Ethical = accepted standards of conduct guiding behavior.

**9.** What is the second step in finding a solution to an ethical dilemma?  
**Answer: Consider alternative courses of action**  
Why: Step 1 = identify/define the problem; Step 2 = consider alternatives.

**10.** How can agency problems be reduced through corporate control?  
**Answer: Executive compensation**  
Why: Stock/option pay aligns managers with owners and can encourage value-creating risk.  
**Wrong trap:** Setting strict goals ≠ solving agency problems.

## Quick Memory Hooks (Unit 2)
- Investments ≠ financial management ≠ institutions  
- Firm goal = **owner wealth**  
- R&D worth it? → **investment** decision  
- After IPO trading → **secondary** market  
- Insurance → **contractual**; company retirement money → **pension fund**  
- Personal income → **coincident**  
- Ethical = standards of conduct  
- Ethical dilemma step 2 = **alternatives**  
- Agency fix via control = **executive compensation** (equity pay)
