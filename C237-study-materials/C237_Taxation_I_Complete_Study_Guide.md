# TOPIC 1 & 7: Nature, Purpose, Scope & Tax Research

## What Is a Tax?
- Payment to government — **not voluntary**, no specific benefit to you individually
- Imposed to fund government (Constitution gives Congress power to tax)
- **16th Amendment (1913)** — Congress can tax **income**

## Three Tax Rate Structures ★ MEMORIZE

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Progressive** | Tax rate **increases** as income increases (marginal rate rises) | Federal & state **income tax** |
| **Regressive** | Tax rate **decreases** as income rises (hits low earners harder) | **Social Security** & **Medicare** (cap/cutoff at higher income) |
| **Proportional (flat)** | Every dollar taxed at **same rate** | **Sales tax**, **excise tax** (e.g., per gallon gas tax) |

**Progressive definition:** Marginal tax rate increases as tax base increases — tax by "chunks."

## Evaluating a Tax System — Five Criteria ★ MEMORIZE

| Criterion | What it means |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Sufficiency** | Government collects enough revenue to pay its bills (deficit = not sufficient) |
| **Equity** | Fairness — **horizontal** (same income → same tax) vs **vertical** (higher income → more ability to pay) |
| **Certainty** | Taxpayers know **where, when, how much** to pay |
| **Convenience** | Easy to collect without undue hardship (payroll withholding, sales tax at register) |
| **Economy / Simplicity** | Low cost to comply (for taxpayer and government) |

## Social Security & Medicare ★ MEMORIZE NUMBERS

| Who | Social Security | Medicare | Combined note |
|-----|-----------------|----------|---------------|
| **Employee** | **6.2%** | **1.45%** | Employer pays matching 6.2% + 1.45% |
| **Self-employed** | Pay **both sides** | Pay **both sides** | **15.3%** total (SS + Medicare) |

- May be called **OASDI** (Old Age, Survivors, Disability Insurance) = same as Social Security
- **Regressive** — at certain income levels you stop paying SS; Medicare % drops
- **2024 example:** SS stops on income above ~$168,600 (concept matters more than exact number on test)

## Employee vs Independent Contractor
- **Employee:** employer withholds all taxes
- **Independent contractor:** self-employed — pay own taxes (15.3%), can deduct business expenses, but responsible for all tax

## Statute of Limitations ★ MEMORIZE

| Period | When |
|--------|------|
| **3 years** | General rule — IRS can audit/correct |
| **6 years** | If you **underreport income by more than 25%** |
| **Indefinite** | **No return filed** OR **fraudulent return** |

**When clock starts:** Later of (1) due date of return (15th day of 4th month / April 15) OR (2) actual filing date if filed late

## Civil Penalties ★ KNOW CHART

**Taxpayer:**
- **Failure to file:** **5%** of tax owed per month (up to 25%); **no penalty if no tax due**
- **Failure to pay:** **0.5%** per month on unpaid balance
- **Fraud:** **75%** of liability

**Tax preparer (willful understatement):** Greater of **$5,000** OR **75% of income** derived from preparing that return

## Tax Research Process ★ MEMORIZE ORDER
1. **Facts** (ALWAYS FIRST)
2. **Issue** (legal question)
3. **Locate relevant authority**
4. **Document**

**Open facts vs closed facts:** **Open facts** = planning future transaction (more advantageous — structure for best outcome). **Closed facts** = transaction already done — only determine liability.

## Sources of Tax Law ★ MEMORIZE HIERARCHY

| Level | Source |
|-------|--------|
| Top | **U.S. Constitution** (16th Amendment = income tax) |
| Legislative | **Internal Revenue Code** (Congress) |
| Administrative | **Treasury Regulations** (Dept. of Treasury) |
| Judicial | **Court decisions** |
| Lesser IRS primary | Revenue rulings, revenue procedures |
| **Private Letter Ruling** | Taxpayer asks IRS how a specific transaction will be taxed — applies **only to that taxpayer** |
| **Technical Advice Memorandum (TAM)** | **IRS agent in field** asks IRS how to handle audit issue |
| **Circular 230** | Rules for **practicing before the IRS** |

**Primary vs secondary:** Primary = from government (argue in court). Secondary = textbooks, journals (explain only — never argue to court).

## Business Entities ★ MEMORIZE

| Type | Tax treatment |
|------|---------------|
| **C Corporation** | **Non-flow-through** — entity files corporate return, **21% flat tax**; dividends taxed again to shareholders = **double taxation** |
| **Partnership, S Corp, LLC (pass-through)** | **Flow-through** — entity issues **K-1**; individuals pay on personal return — **taxed once** |

**Test trick:** Non-flow-through = **C Corporation only**

## Tax Avoidance vs Evasion
- **Avoidance** = legal planning to minimize tax
- **Evasion** = **willful** fraud — **criminal** (e.g., Al Capone)

## IRS Audits (3 types)
1. **Correspondence** — mail, 1–2 items
2. **Office examination** — go to IRS office
3. **Field examination** — IRS at business (most extensive)

## Pay-as-You-Go & Filing
- Individual tax year ends **December 31**
- Return due **15th day of 4th month** after year-end (usually **April 15**); next business day if weekend/holiday
- **Extension to file** = 6 months — **NOT extension to pay**
- Underpayment penalty avoided if pay **90% of current year** OR **100% of prior year** liability
- Estimated taxes (self-employed): quarterly — Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15

# TOPIC 2: Individual Income Tax Formula ★ HEAVY EXAM TOPIC

## The Four Components ★ WRITE ON WHITEBOARD

1. **Filing status**
2. **Income** — everything from whatever source unless government says don't count
3. **Deduction** — standard OR itemized (take **whichever is bigger** — only ONE)
4. **Tax liability** — use correct progressive tax chart for filing status

## Income Types (3 levels)
- **Gross income** → minus **FOR (above-the-line)** deductions → **Adjusted Gross Income (AGI)**
- **AGI** → minus **FROM (below-the-line)** deduction → **Taxable income**
- **Taxable income** → tax charts → liability → minus credits & prepayments → refund or owe

## Gross Income Rule ★ MEMORIZE
**"Everything from whatever source derived"** unless excluded.

### DO NOT COUNT (common on test):
- **Gifts**
- **Inheritances**
- **Interest from municipal bonds** (tax-free at federal level)
- **Alimony / child support / spousal support** (post-2018 rules)
- Life insurance proceeds (may have other taxes)
- Most personal injury settlements (not punitive damages)

### DO COUNT:
- Wages, interest (non-municipal), gambling winnings
- **Illegal income** — still taxable
- Constructive receipt — if entitled to money with no restrictions, count it even if not cashed

## Unrealized vs Realized vs Recognized ★ MEMORIZE

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Unrealized** | Value on paper — **no tax** |
| **Realized** | Cash in hand — transaction completed |
| **Recognized** | Must report on tax return |

You will have **more realized than recognized** (municipal bond interest realized but not recognized).

## Ordinary vs Capital Income
- **Ordinary** — wages, progressive charts (10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, 37%)
- **Capital / preferential** — long-term capital gains: **0%, 15%, or 20%** (hold **more than 1 year** = 1 day longer than a year)

## FOR vs FROM Deductions ★ ONE WORD CHANGES ANSWER

| | FOR (above-the-line) | FROM (below-the-line) |
|--|----------------------|------------------------|
| Timing | Before AGI | After AGI |
| Examples | Business expenses, rental expenses, **half of self-employment tax** | **Standard** or **itemized** |

Think FOR = **business expense**

## Standard Deduction 2024 (given on exam — know concept)
- Married filing joint / qualified widow: **$29,200**
- Head of household: **$21,900**
- Single / married filing separate: **$14,600**
- **90%+ of people take standard** because amounts increased in 2018

## Progressive Tax Chart Math ★ PRACTICE
Example: Taxable income falls in bracket → base tax on lower chunks + % on excess over threshold.
**Use the chart for YOUR filing status** (single, HOH, MFJ, MFS).

# TOPIC 3: Gains & Losses

## Capital Assets — Defined by EXCLUSION ★ MEMORIZE NOT CAPITAL

For **business**, NOT capital assets:
1. **Inventory**
2. **Supplies**
3. **Real property used specifically in the business**

If not capital → **ordinary/non-capital asset**

**Capital assets:** stock held for investment, personal house/car, collectibles bought to appreciate, business real estate held for investment (not used in operations)

## Basis ★ MEMORIZE
**What it cost + what it cost to put in use**
- Purchase price + delivery + installation + setup to run
- **NOT** ongoing maintenance (that's expense)
- Ignore fair market value in basis questions — only cost to acquire and place in service

## Holding Period ★ MEMORIZE
- **Long-term:** held **more than 1 year** (366th day in non-leap year; 367th in leap year)
- **Short-term:** 1 year or less → taxed as **ordinary income**

## Netting
- Net long-term gains vs long-term losses
- Net short-term gains vs short-term losses
- Then net long vs short

## Capital Loss Limitation (individuals)
- Can deduct only **$3,000** of capital losses against ordinary income per year
- Excess **carries forward** indefinitely
- **Cannot** take loss on personal use property (house, car) even if sold at loss
- Cannot take loss on sale to related family member at loss

## Gift vs Inheritance Basis ★ MEMORIZE

| | Gift (donor alive) | Inheritance (after death) |
|--|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **Basis** | **Donor's basis** (carryover) | **Stepped-up basis** = FMV at death |
| **Holding period** | Donor's holding period | Always **long-term** |

## Expense vs Depreciate
- **Expense** — used up within the year (wages, utilities, repairs, supplies)
- **Depreciate** — big-ticket items lasting multiple years (machines, buildings, vehicles)

## Depreciation Order ★ MEMORIZE
1. **Section 179** — expense instead of depreciate (limit **$1,220,000** in 2024)
2. **Bonus depreciation** — **60%** in 2024 (declining over time)
3. **MACRS** — default system

## MACRS Personal Property ★ MEMORIZE 5 vs 7 YEARS
| 5-year | 7-year |
|--------|--------|
| Autos, light trucks, computers | Office furniture, fixtures, equipment |

**When in doubt, pick 5.** Chart given — **half-year convention** year 1 = **20%** for 5-year asset (not 40% because half year).

## MACRS Real Property
- **Land NEVER depreciates** — subtract land value from purchase price
- Charts: **residential** vs **nonresidential** — if not specified, assume **nonresidential**
- **Mid-month convention** year 1; straight-line after
- Assume **2024** purchases for exam charts

# TOPIC 4 & 5: Deductions & Itemized Deductions

## Six Itemized Deductions ★ MEMORIZE — WRITE ON WHITEBOARD

1. **Medical** — above **7.5% of AGI** floor
2. **Charity** — qualified **501(c)(3)** domestic charities only
3. **Mortgage interest** — first and **second** home
4. **Property (real estate) tax** — first and second home
5. **Sales tax** OR **income tax** (pick one, not both)
6. (Sales and income tax are the two tax options — count as part of the six)

**Mnemonic:** Medical, Charity, Mortgage, Property tax, Sales/Income tax

## Medical Deduction Math ★ MEMORIZE 7.5%
```
Floor = AGI × 7.5%
Deductible medical = Medical expenses − Floor (if positive)
```

Not deductible: cosmetic surgery, federally illegal drugs (marijuana), OTC meds (generally)

## Charity Limits ★ MEMORIZE
- Must be **registered 501(c)(3)** — not homeless person, neighbor, political campaigns
- **Cash to public charities:** up to **60% of AGI**
- **Capital gain property:** up to **30% of AGI**
- **Ordinary income property** (used clothes/furniture): up to **50% of AGI**
- Excess carries forward

## Mortgage Interest
- First and second home only
- **Home equity interest** deductible only if used to **improve** the home

## State/Local Taxes (SALT)
- **$10,000 cap** on property + sales/income tax deduction combined (know concept)

## Miscellaneous Itemized (only one left)
- **Gambling losses** — only to extent of **gambling winnings** (reduce winnings to zero, not below)
- Military moving expenses (pursuant to orders) — rare
- **No** tax prep, hobbies, hobby expenses (eliminated 2018)

## Business Deductions
Must be **ordinary, necessary, and reasonable**
- **NOT deductible:** bribes, fines, penalties, political contributions, lobbying
- **Meals:** only **50%** deductible when business-related
- **Mileage for business:** $0.67/mile (2024 reference); commute home-to-work NOT deductible

## Accounting Methods ★ MEMORIZE
| Cash | Accrual |
|------|---------|
| Income when **received** | Income when **earned** (all events test) |
| Expense when **paid** | Expense when **incurred** |

Large corps generally must use accrual.

## Discharge of Indebtedness
- **Insolvent / bankruptcy** — generally **not taxable**
- **Solvent** — cancellation of debt is **taxable income**

# TOPIC 6: Filing Status, Credits, Final Steps

## Five Filing Statuses ★ MEMORIZE RULES

| Status | Key rules |
|--------|-----------|
| **Married filing joint (MFJ)** | Married on **last day of year** (Dec 31); exception: spouse died during year can still file MFJ that year; **joint and several liability** |
| **Married filing separate (MFS)** | Married Dec 31 but file separately; avoids joint liability; **no tax benefit**; same standard as single |
| **Qualified widow(er)** | Spouse died; **next 2 years** after death; **not remarried** + **dependent must be a CHILD** (not parent) |
| **Head of household (HOH)** | Unmarried or **abandoned spouse** (lived apart 6+ months, paid >half home cost) + **dependent** + paid >half cost of keeping home |
| **Single** | Default — unmarried, no dependents |

### HOH Dependents (broader than qualified widow):
- Children, qualifying relatives (live with you 6+ months, you support)
- **Parents** — don't have to live with you if you provide >half support
- **Non-relative** — must live with you **entire 12 months**, income < $5,050 (2024), you provide >half support

**Practice:** Married Feb, baby May, divorced Nov → **not married Dec 31** → custodial parent = **HOH**, other = **single**

## Tax Credits Overview ★ MEMORIZE ALL RULES

Credits **reduce tax owed** (dollar for dollar), not taxable income.

### Refundable vs Nonrefundable
| Type | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Refundable** | Can go below zero → **refund check** | **Earned Income Credit (EIC)** |
| **Nonrefundable** | Reduces tax to zero only; no refund of excess | **Lifetime Learning Credit** |
| **Partially refundable** | Some refundable portion | American Opportunity (know concept) |

## Child & Dependent Credits ★ MEMORIZE

| Credit | Rules | Amount |
|--------|-------|--------|
| **Child Tax Credit** | Under **17** at Dec 31 | **$2,000** per child |
| **Qualifying dependent credit** | Age **17–24** full-time student OR other dependents | **$500** each |
| **Child & Dependent Care Credit** | Kids under **13** (or incapacitated dependent); parent **working or looking for work** | **$3,000** one child / **$6,000** two or more (max) |

Dependent must be U.S. citizen, national, or resident alien.

## Education Credits ★ MEMORIZE

| | American Opportunity | Lifetime Learning |
|--|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Years** | First **4 years** post-secondary (bachelor's) | Any level |
| **Student** | At least **half-time** | Any |
| **Other** | No **drug felony** conviction | — |
| **Amount** | **$2,500** per student | **$2,000** total |
| **Refundable?** | Partially | **Nonrefundable** |

If qualify for both → take **whichever is bigger** (not both).

## Earned Income Credit (EIC) ★ MEMORIZE
- For **low-income wage earners** — goal is **refund check**
- Must have **earned income** from a job
- Age **25–65** OR have **qualifying child**
- Investment/passive income must be **≤ $11,600** (2024)
- **Refundable**

## Ordinary vs Preferential Tax
- **Ordinary income** — progressive charts
- **Long-term capital gains** — 0%, 15%, or 20%
- Short-term capital gains = ordinary rates
